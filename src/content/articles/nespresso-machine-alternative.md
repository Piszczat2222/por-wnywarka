## Why Nespresso Still Commands the Price

Nespresso turned espresso into an appliance anyone can run before a Zoom call: insert capsule, press button, get a consistent shot. At **~$150–200** for many Vertuo and Original machines, the sticker looks fine — until you add the **capsule tax** every morning.

The brand also sells frothers, limited flavors, and a gift-ready unboxing moment. If convenience and consistency beat hobby tinkering, Nespresso still owns that lane.

## How We Compared These Two

We compared **brew method**, **taste control**, **milk drinks**, **ongoing cost**, and **mess**. Capsule machines win on speed and guest-proof operation. Budget 20-bar machines win when you want real espresso economics and a steam wand without buying an Aeroccino separately.

Key factors:

- **Pods vs beans** — Nespresso locks you into capsules; Amazon espresso machines use ground coffee
- **Learning curve** — dialing grind and tamp vs one button
- **Milk** — many budget units include a wand; Nespresso often upsells frothing
- **Cost over 6 months** — capsules add up faster than a bag of beans

## What You Give Up (and What You Keep)

You give up pop-in-pod simplicity, Nespresso's flavor catalog, and the brand's repair / club ecosystem. Puck cleanup and a decent grind matter more than with capsules.

You keep espresso, Americanos, and milk drinks at a fraction of the per-cup cost, plus the option to upgrade beans without changing machines. For most daily drinkers who will spend ten minutes learning, that trade wins.
